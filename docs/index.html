The grade

One grade per market, scored out of 100 from two ingredients. This is the same scheme the NFL card uses, with every constant re-measured on college data.

Simulated win probability
70 of 100 points		 How often this pick wins across 20,000 simulations. Scaled so 40% earns nothing and 60% or better earns the full 70. The dominant term.
Value over the price
30 of 100 points		 Simulated probability minus what the price implies, after stripping the book's cut. Scaled so no edge earns nothing and 8 points or more earns the full 30.

Bands. A at 70 or higher, B 55 to 69, C 40 to 54, D under 40.

Minimum value gates. Because win probability carries most of the weight, a heavy favorite could grade well while offering nothing over the price. Under 2 points of edge cannot grade A, under 1 point cannot grade above C, under half a point is always D.

The fourth gate, which the NFL card does not need

The three gates above assume the simulated win probability can be trusted. In college it cannot, and that is measured rather than suspected. The model's median simulated cover probability is 60.2%. Games it puts at 3 to 6 points of edge actually covered 50.7% over 880 backtested games; at 6 to 10 points, 49.1% over 466. A 60% simulated cover is a coin flip in reality.

Run this scheme with no fourth gate and it grades 115 of 154 spreads an A, on a strategy that backtested at 47.4% in weeks 1 to 4. Moving the scale endpoints only changes how many A grades get printed; it cannot make them mean anything. So: a market whose strategy backtested with its whole 95% interval below break-even cannot grade above D. A star on a grade means it was capped, and the raw score is still shown on the game so the cap is visible rather than silent.

The gate lifts on its own. Re-run the cfb-backtest workflow, update the record, and every grade recalculates.

The simulation

Every game runs 20,000 times rather than through a single formula. Each run makes two draws.

Our own uncertaintyWe do not know the true line, only our estimate. Each run pulls a true line from a distribution centred on our number with a 7.0-point spread. The NFL card uses 3.0. Ours is more than double because that is what the data says: the standard deviation of our number minus the market's is 7.0 points.
The game itselfGiven that line, the margin is drawn with a standard deviation of about 15.0 points, against the NFL's 13.2, widening for high totals and tightening for low ones. Totals are drawn separately at 13.3 points, against the NFL's 10.4.

The margin tilt is fitted, not assumed: absolute residual = 0.0541 x total + 7.35 over 762 FBS games from 2025, normalised at a total of 52. Wind is not adjusted for, because there is no historical college weather on hand to measure it.
